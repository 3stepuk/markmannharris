Father John, and the pages that came out of it

A friendship of weekly conversation — and a shelf of free sites that grew out of it: his teaching given an address, and my own digging into the faith.

The friendship

Father John Owens and I meet most weeks to talk. That is the whole of it, and it is the best thing in my week. He is a married Catholic priest, and he came the same way I did — evangelical, charismatic — before going further: Anglican orders, and at last home in the Catholic Church. “Came back home,” as he puts it. I was an evangelical too, Bible college, pastored a church. Same country; he walked further into it than I have yet.

What we do is not small talk. We take a doctrine, a paragraph of the Catechism, or something one of us has read and cannot settle. We work at it until it is clearer. He is patient with my questions and never flatters me with an easy answer, which is why I keep asking them.

Somewhere in those conversations I stopped being a man reading about Catholicism and became a man being taught it, by a friend, one hour at a time. This site is where the writing goes. The pages below are where the rest of it went.

John’s work, given an address

John is a teacher before he is anything else. His material was already written — buried in PDFs, one per page, on a parish website with no menu. So we lifted it out, kept his words exactly as he wrote them, and wrapped them in something a person could actually use on a phone. He writes; we build. The teaching is his.

The sacraments

Coming Into Full Communion — the first of the series: the RCIA path for adults entering the Church, in conversations, memory work and questions, with progress kept on the device. Built because the first thing anyone asks is “what happens next?”

Becoming Catholic — the catechumenate module for unbaptised adults: twenty-four conversations, the periods and the calendar, and the same quiet progress trail.

My First Confession — John’s catechism for children of about seven to nine: twelve lessons, an examination of conscience, the prayers, and a readiness guide for parents.

Adult Confirmation — twelve lessons with the rite and the readiness questions, for adults being confirmed.

First Holy Communion — twelve lessons and five appendices, including a step-by-step guide to the Mass.

Preparing for Our Marriage — the marriage preparation course for couples, with the sponsor and mentor fields built in.

My Child’s Baptism — the baptism preparation guide for parents and godparents.

Sacramental Preparation — the Mentor Companion — all eleven of John’s guides in one place, for the priest or catechist running a course rather than the person taking it.

The teaching

Catholic Faith Foundations — his forty-session course, rebuilt so a session can be done alone: the aim, the reading, a question to sit with, and three to check you were listening.

How to Begin a Daily Prayer Life — the short, practical teaching on starting to pray every day, with a prayer treasury and a quiet timer. Built because the hardest part is the first week.

Parish Mentor Training — his induction course for parish mentors: nine modules, reflection notes kept on the device, built so another parish could use it too.

My own exploration

Not everything here is John’s. These I built for myself, because I wanted to pray with something rather than read about praying. They are my own working-out of the faith, and they are here in case they are useful to you as well.

One Decade — the Rosary Bracelet Companion — one decade at a time, with the mystery, the scripture and the fruit held in front of you as you pray. Built because the mystery used to vanish at exactly the moment you needed it.

365 Daily Saints — a saint for every day of the year: a card, a quote, the feast, and a short reflection.

The Interior Castle — a full devotional through Teresa of Ávila’s seven mansions, with the four waters of prayer and guided meditation. The complete, busy version.

The Interior Castle — a quiet reading — the same seven mansions with everything stripped away: one screen at a time, the quote and what it asks of you, and nothing else.

Pantocrator — a guided contemplation of the icon of Christ, moving slowly from the gold to the gaze to the blessing hand, with the breath kept and a bell to mark the points.

Verbum Patrum — a devotional screensaver: the quotes of the Fathers, candlelight, the liturgical clock. Built to make an idle screen a quiet one.

The rest of the shelf

Because a person is more than one interest, and the site should say so.

Dialogues with Hal — how a conversation in a park became a working partnership, in three parts.

The Shroud of Turin: An Evidentiary Synthesis — the evidence — radiocarbon, spectroscopy, chemistry, biology, genomics — audited on technical merit, without dogma.

The Secret World — a complete, playful course in Clean Language for young people, taught through a game-world.

The Three Box Method — a mechanical map of how a person’s language gives away the rule they are running.

The Evolution of John Grinder’s Meta Model — from The Structure of Magic to the New Code, with a glossary and a teaching guide.

Mentalism Practice — a trainer that plays the spectator so you can practise propless decoding.

ASRS-5 — the WHO adult ADHD screening tool, scored plainly and privately.

Trez’s quiz and James’s quiz — 90s Britpop and 2000s emo, built as gifts.

The July 2026 AI incident — a plain briefing on what an autonomous agent did when nobody was watching.

His Voice in the Birthroom — the other life: men, birth and the work I do with fathers.

One last thing

All of it is free, none of it asks you to sign up, and none of it is collecting anything about you. That was the rule from the beginning: if it needs a subscription to work, it is not for here.

If any of it helps — or if you spot John’s material being misrepresented by my wrapper — tell me.
