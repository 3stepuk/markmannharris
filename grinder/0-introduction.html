John Grinder – co-founder of Neuro-Linguistic Programming (NLP) – has continually refined his understanding of language and its role in human change over the past five decades. Nowhere is this more evident than in the evolution of the Meta Model, a set of linguistic patterns and interventions first introduced in The Structure of Magic, Volume I (1975) and later streamlined in the New Code of NLP as the “Verbal Package” (circa 2001). This report traces Grinder’s evolving thought in his own words (and co-authored works), from the Meta Model’s early development through transitional refinements to its New Code re-design. Along the way, we will compare the original Meta Model with the New Code Verbal Package, examine Grinder’s rationale for simplifying the model (including his critiques of “Classic Code” NLP), and show how the Verbal Package fits into the broader principles of New Code NLP. A glossary of key terms and linguistic distinctions is provided to clarify Grinder’s terminology across different works. In conclusion, a teaching-oriented guide suggests how to adapt the Verbal Package for beginners – for example, birth professionals seeking practical communication tools – in plain language while honoring the depth of Grinder’s insights.

Throughout this document, direct quotations from Grinder’s books and articles are used to capture his thinking in his own voice. Citations include the source title, year, and page references where available. This compilation is intended as a comprehensive learning archive for those aspiring to deep mastery of NLP’s linguistic core, enabling a future expert to appreciate how Grinder’s ideas developed and how they can be applied in modern professional contexts.
