Mid, with; wif, woman

How a phone call, and the meaning of a word, turned nursing into midwifery — and why both prepared me for this.

7 November 2026

I trained as a nurse in 1991 and as a midwife in 1993. But that isn't where the story starts. It starts with being pulled in the wrong direction.

Nursing taught me quickly where the work was. It was on the dementia wards and in the care homes, in the ordinary business of being with someone who is having a hard day. But the further I went, the more of my time went on managing — rotas, beds, paperwork — and the less of it went on being with anybody. I was depressed about it. Not dramatically. Just a slow realisation that I had walked towards the thing I wanted and ended up standing behind a desk instead of beside a person.

Then Dave Smith rang.

Dave was the first male midwife in Warwickshire. He lived with us for a while, and he knew me well enough to hear it in my voice. He said: mate, become a midwife.

And then he told me the word.

Midwife. Mid, meaning with. Wif, meaning woman. You couldn't be a midwife without being with woman — the word itself wouldn't let you.

That was the whole argument. Not a career plan. A definition.

So I did it. Midwifery took me into birth rooms, and that is where I stayed for the rest of my clinical career. Later came teaching, and then work in secure mental health hospitals. And now I am a personal assistant: self-employed, part-time, working with people in their own homes and out in the world.

People ask how that is a straight line. It isn't. But every part of it prepared me for this one.

  • The wards and the care homes taught me that the task is rarely the point. The person is.
  • Birth rooms taught me to be with someone in the middle of something enormous, without taking it over.
  • Teaching taught me to explain without condescending, and to notice the moment someone hasn't understood.
  • Secure mental health taught me to stay steady, keep my own reaction out of the room, and know exactly where the edges of my role are.
  • Being an Appropriate Adult taught me to sit with someone inside a system that was not built for them.

A personal assistant does all of that in one afternoon.

The thing I keep coming back to is Dave's definition. Mid — with. It is the same word for what I do now. I am not doing things to people. I am with them while they do them.

I thought, when I was managing, that I had taken a wrong turn. I hadn't. I had been collecting the things I would need later.

First of these. I'll write when there is something worth saying — about the work, about working with people, and about what I'm learning. Never about a client, unless they have said I can.
