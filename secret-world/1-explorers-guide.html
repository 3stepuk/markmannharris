Module 1 — The Explorer's Guide

How to Help Your Friends Explore Their Mind Without Changing Their Map

Did you know that every single person has a Secret World inside their head?

When your friend says, "I have a heavy knot in my stomach about the math test," or "I'm boiling with anger," they aren't just using words. In their Secret World, there is an actual knot. There is an actual pot of boiling water.

Most people try to help by jumping into their friend's world and messing with things. They say, "Just untie the knot!" or "Turn off the stove!" But you can't push buttons in someone else's world. If you try, you usually just break things or make your friend feel misunderstood.

This guide teaches you how to be a Clean Explorer. A Clean Explorer never touches the other person's stuff.

Instead, you use special Clean Questions to help your friend shine a flashlight on their own map. When they can see their world clearly, their own brain figures out how to fix the glitches all by itself.

1. The Explorer's Golden Rules

How to be a Good Guide

The "No Littering" Rule

Adults call this: Non-Contamination

When you explore your friend's world, you must leave no trace of your own stuff. If they say, "I'm stuck in a dark cave," you don't say, "Are there bats in there?"

(Because now you just put bats in their cave!). You only use the exact words they give you.

The "Camera Angle" Rule

Adults call this: Epistemological Distance

Sometimes your friend's world gets scary or overwhelming. If they say, "The monster is right in my face," they are too close to the screen. You can help them zoom the camera out to a safe distance by asking where they are standing while they look at it.

From far away, it's just a game level; they can look at it without being scared.

The "Live Map" Rule

Adults call this: Psychoactive Landscape

Their Secret World is alive. It's like a video game that renders in real-time. If you ask a question about a "heavy rock," the moment they look at the rock, it might suddenly turn into a balloon or melt into sand. Your questions actually make the world move and change!

2. The Secret Map

What You Will Find Inside Here is a list of things you will find in someone's Secret World, and what the boring adult words for them are.

Landmarks (Adults call this: Nodes)

A specific thing in their world.

It could be a feeling ("a tight chest"), an object ("a brick wall"), or a state ("a dark cloud"). Landmarks are the building blocks of their level.

The Trail (Adults call this:

Vectors)

The invisible footprints that show where things are moving. It's the path of where something came from, or where it is going next.

The Glitch (Adults call

this: Problem Loop)

A trap where your friend keeps walking in circles. For example:

"I get stressed, so I stop doing my homework, which makes me more stressed, so I keep not doing it." It's a glitch in the game that keeps repeating.

2. The Secret Map

The Safe Zone (Adults

call this: Resource Space)

A secret, safe area on their map. Even if their world feels like a raging storm, there is always a quiet, strong place hidden somewhere (like "a solid feeling in my feet"). Your job is to help them find it.

Body Clues (Adults call this: Somatic Endpoints)

When the Secret World changes, the real-world body reacts. If your friend suddenly takes a huge, deep breath, or their shoulders relax, or their eyes look up—that is a Body Clue! It means a door just unlocked in their map.

The Stuck Trap (Adults

call this: Double Bind)

A puzzle where both options seem bad. "If I tell the teacher, my friends will be mad. If I don't tell the teacher, I'll get in trouble." They are frozen. You don't solve it for them; you just help them map out the trap until they spot the hidden exit.

3. The Explorer's Toolkit

The Clean Questions

You only need a few tools to map the world. Ask these questions slowly, using your friend's exact words, and wait for them to look around their world and answer.

Tools 1 & 2: Zoom In and Pin It Down

1. The Magnifying Glass

To see details

The Question: "And what kind of [X] is that [X]?"

How it works: If they say they have a "headache," you ask, "And what kind of headache is that headache?"

It forces them to zoom in. (They might say, "It's a spiky, red headache.")

2. The Map Pin

To find coordinates

The Question: "And where is [X]?" or "And does [X]

have a size or a shape?"

How it works: It pins the landmark to a specific spot.

"Where is that spiky red headache?" (They might say, "It's right behind my left eye.")

Tools 3, 4 & 5: Look Around and Track the Trail

3. The Flashlight

To look around

The Question: "And is there

anything else about [X]?"

How it works: Shines a light on the edges of the landmark to see what else is hiding there.

4. The Rewind Button

To find the trigger

The Question: "And what

happens just before [X]?"

How it works: Tracks the trail backward to see how the glitch started.

5. The Fast-Forward

Button To see the future

The Question: "And then what

happens?"

How it works: Tracks the trail forward to see what the glitch causes next.

6. The Quest Compass

To change the level "And when [X] is there, what would you like to happen?"

How it works: This is the most powerful tool. It points them out of the glitch and sets a new objective for their game.

4. Let's Play: A Real Conversation

Here is an example of two 11-year-olds using this guide. Watch how the Explorer never gives advice, but the Friend figures it out anyway!

Friend "I'm so worried about giving my presentation tomorrow. I feel completely frozen." (They revealed a Landmark: 'frozen')

Explorer "And you feel completely frozen.

And when you feel frozen, what kind of frozen is that frozen?" (The Magnifying Glass)

Friend "It's like I'm trapped in a block of ice."

Explorer "And you're trapped in a block of ice. And where is that block of ice?" (The Map Pin)

Friend "It's wrapped all around my legs and my stomach. It makes me feel stuck."

4. Let's Play: A Real Conversation

Explorer "Wrapped all around your legs and your stomach.

And when you're trapped in that block of ice... what would you like to happen?" (The Quest Compass)

Friend "I just want it to melt so I can walk up to the front of the classroom normally."

Explorer "And you want it to melt so you can walk to the front.

And is there anything else about melting?" (The Flashlight)

Friend (Pauses, looks at their hands) "Well... my hands are actually pretty warm right now. Like, I feel a lot of heat in my palms." (A Safe Zone appears!)

Explorer "And you feel a lot of heat in your palms. And when there is heat in your palms, what happens to that block of ice?" (Connecting the Safe Zone to the Landmark)

Friend (Takes a deep breath—a Body Clue!) "The heat kind of pushes down. The ice is cracking a little bit. It's not so tight anymore."

Mission Accomplished!

The Explorer didn't tell the friend to "just relax" or "imagine the class in their underwear." The Explorer just helped map the ice and the heat, and the friend's own Secret World did the rest.
