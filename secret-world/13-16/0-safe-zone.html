Module 0

The Safe Zone

A place on your map that already holds. You don't build it. You find it.

Before you start

What this is. A set of questions that help you notice how you already see something.

  • What it isn't. Not therapy. Not a test. There are no right answers, and nobody marks it.
  • It's your choice, every time. Pass on a question. Stop halfway. Never come back. All fine.
  • Your answers are yours. Nothing here is sent anywhere or saved by anyone else.
  • If something hard comes up, stop, and tell someone you trust. Stopping is always allowed.

1

Everyone's world is different

Ask three people about the same thing. A dog. The sea. A thunderstorm. Then listen to how far apart the answers are.

“Warm and heavy. He leans on your leg.”A dog
“Loud. All teeth and a cold nose.”A dog
“Soft ears. Like a scarf you can hold.”A dog

Three people. One dog. Three completely different worlds.

None of them is wrong, and none of them is the dog. Each one is the dog as that person has it. Once you've seen that, you can't unsee it. The person next to you is walking around inside a world you have never been in.

That is the whole reason this exists.

2

The quiet, strong place

Things on a map move. A worry grows. A mood changes colour. A thought turns into something else while you're looking at it.

Some landmarks don't move. You look at them and they stay. Those are the places where the world goes steady, and where a person can stand.

This is what we mean by a Safe Zone. A place, a person, a pet, a song, a moment — whatever you choose. Every map has one. Nobody installs it. It's found.

In the literature, this is Resource Space.

Their choice, not yours

You don't decide whether it's a good place. The person picks it, and you follow. If they pick something you wouldn't have picked, that's the material — not a mistake to correct.

In the literature, this is Non-Contamination.

Body Clues

You know a Safe Zone has been found because the body says so. A long breath out. Shoulders dropping. Eyes going up and away. That's a door unlocking.

In the literature, this is the Somatic Endpoint.

3

How to model it

Five moves. The same questions you already know, in order.

Move 1 — Choose the place

“And what would you like to explore?”

Don't offer a menu. Wait. Whatever they say first is the place, even if it sounds ordinary — a bench, a nan's kitchen, the smell of a car.

Move 2 — Give it a shape or a sound

“And what kind of [X] is that [X]?”the Magnifying Glass

“And does [X] have a size or a shape?”the Map Pin

A place with a colour, a light or a sound can be found again. A place with nothing on it can't.

Move 3 — Follow the thread

“And where does [X] come from?”

“And what happens just before [X]?”the Rewind

“And then what happens?”the Fast-Forward

Most Safe Zones have a way in — a door, a smell, a sound at the gate. The way in is the useful part.

Move 4 — Find it in the body

“And where do you feel [X]?”

Pin it to a place — chest, feet, hands, throat. That's the Body Clue, and it's how you both know the map has actually moved.

Move 5 — Make a way back

“And what would help you find your way back to [X]?”the Compass

A smell, a word, a sound, a picture. Something small they can carry. It doesn't have to work every time. It only has to be a handle.

If any of this stops being useful, stop. Nothing here needs finishing.

Try it on a place of your own. Three lines is enough.

Write in it if you like. Nothing is sent anywhere.

4

A real conversation

One person mapping a place for another. Watch what the questions do — and what they never do.

Friend

“My nan's kitchen. That's my place.”

Navigator

“And your nan's kitchen. And what kind of kitchen is that kitchen?”the Magnifying Glass

Friend

“It's small. There's a yellow light on and the kettle's always just boiled.”

Navigator

“A small kitchen, and a yellow light, and the kettle just boiled. And is there anything else about that yellow light?”the Flashlight

Friend

“It's warm on the ceiling. Like the whole room is glowing a bit.”

Navigator

“And the whole room is glowing a bit. And where do you feel that glow?”the Map Pin, to the body

Friend

“In my chest. It goes loose.”A long breath out. Shoulders drop. A Body Clue.

Navigator

“And in your chest, and it goes loose. And what happens just before you get to that kitchen?”the Rewind

Friend

“The front door. You can smell toast before you're even in.”

Navigator

“And you can smell toast before you're even in. And when you're standing in that kitchen, then what happens?”the Fast-Forward

Friend

“I just put my bag down. I don't have to be anything.”

Navigator

“And you put your bag down, and you don't have to be anything. And what would help you find your way back to that kitchen when you're not there?”the Compass

Friend

“The smell of toast. I could just think about toast.”

Navigator

“And the smell of toast.”Nothing added.

That is the whole craft. Three things the Navigator never does: never says “that sounds lovely”; never adds anything that wasn't there; never fills a silence.

5

When to use it

When nothing is wrong

The easiest time, and the best time. You learn the questions on a place that already holds, so nobody is learning two things at once.

Before something hard

A test, a hospital appointment, a new school, a party you're dreading. Map the place first, so there's somewhere to stand when you get there.

Just to practise

Ten minutes on a bench, a song, a kitchen. Every conversation you have makes the next question easier to ask.

6

For the adult

The material is written for the young person. This page is the one to read first, and the reasons are practical.

The questions are learned on easy material. Asking “what kind of kitchen is that kitchen?” about a place that holds is a different job from asking it about something that hurts. Practise the first before you need the second.

The person ends up with somewhere to stand. A mapped Safe Zone is a place, with a shape, a body feeling and a way back. It is useful on its own, before anything is wrong.

Don't run it on them. If they ask what you're doing, tell them. If they'd rather not, that is the answer.
