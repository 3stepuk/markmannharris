Module 7

The Translation Matrix

Every word in the programme, set against the clinical term it stands for.

Why this page exists

The metaphor is not decoration. Every term in it stands for something specific in Symbolic Modelling, and this page is the key to both directions.

If you are learning the method, this is the page that keeps the play honest. If you already know the clinical vocabulary, this is where you check that nothing was lost in translation.

The map

Clinical termThe Secret WorldWhat it does
The landscape
Autogenic Narrative / Metaphor (a self-generated inner landscape)The Secret MapEveryone has a unique, custom-built world. They are the only one who lives in it.
Perceptual Filtering / Constructivism (the map is not the territory)Three worlds, one dogTwo people can hold the same thing in completely different ways, and neither is wrong.
Non-Contamination (excluding the facilitator's assumptions)The “No Littering” ruleDon't drop your own ideas, advice or vocabulary into someone else's world.
Epistemological Distance (perceptual distance from the phenomenon)The camera angleIf it is too close to look at, step back. From further away it can be studied instead of survived.
Psychoactive Landscape (a space that responds to attention)The World Engine / live mapThe inner world isn't a static picture. Looking at something with the right question makes it re-render.
The pieces
Node (an atomic metaphorical anchor)The LandmarkTurns an abstract feeling into a concrete thing — a heavy rock, a dark cloud — that can be located and changed.
Vector (a direction or line of implication)The thread / Invisible StringsTracks where a feeling came from, where it's going, or how one landmark is wired to another.
Problem Loop (a closed, self-referencing threat circuit)The GlitchNormalises being stuck. Nothing is broken; the system is repeating.
Resource Space (latent generative areas)The Safe ZoneA place on the map that already holds. It is noticed, not installed. Module 0 models it before there is anything to fix.
Somatic Endpoint (physical manifestation of a structure)Body CluesWatch for real-world updates — a long breath, dropping shoulders — to know the system has processed the change.
Binding / Double Binding (two opposing forces locking the system)The Glitch TrapParalysed by two bad options. Don't force a choice; look at the space between them.
The questions
Developing (attributes) — “And what kind of [X] is that [X]?”The Magnifying GlassMakes the engine render high-resolution detail of a confusing feeling.
Developing (location) — “And whereabouts is that [X]?”The Map PinStops a feeling being a vague, overwhelming cloud by pinning it to a coordinate.
Moving attention — “And is there anything else about [X]?”The FlashlightLights the edges around a landmark, where the detail you haven't heard yet is hiding.
Temporal (past) — “And what happens just before [X]?”The RewindRuns the footage back to the moment the loop started.
Temporal (future) — “And then what happens?”The Fast-ForwardRuns it forward to see what the loop causes next.
Outcome pivot — “And when [X], what would you like to have happen?”The CompassTurns from the problem to a direction, and the system starts working on that instead.
Working alone and looking forward
Clean Space Protocol (moving to multiple geographic coordinates)The roomPhysically move to a new spot to change the angle — bypassing stuckness through movement.
The Six Steps Architecture (iterative bypass of analytical resistance)Drawing it outDrawing left to right on a page mirrors the steps of tracking, pivoting and integrating.
Landscape Maturing (allowing structural shifts to integrate)Letting it settlePacing control: don't rush away the moment it feels better. Wait while the new map saves over the old one.
Ecological Future Pacing (testing the resource in a future context)The door backAsk what they will notice tomorrow, and place the new Safe Zone in that future so it loads when they get there.
