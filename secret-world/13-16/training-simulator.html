The Training Simulator

Four weeks of practice in noticing. You can do all of it without a single real conversation.

What this trains

The best facilitators don't just ask good questions. They notice things early — the exact word that holds a problem together, the moment someone's map shifts, the small change in breathing before anything is said.

That is a skill, and it is trainable. The training ground is other people's conversations: podcasts, videos, interviews, the bus.

Part 1

Three things to track

1. The words

Treat their words as physical objects. If they say “a massive concrete wall”, see a massive concrete wall. If your brain translates it to “they mean a big problem”, you have lost the map. Concrete is cold and hard and scrapes your hands. “A big problem” is a boring idea.

2. The space

People draw their maps in the air while they talk. Look down and left for “heavy doubt”, point up for “my goal”, and they have just given you coordinates. Pin them where they pointed.

3. The behaviour

How a thing behaves in the map is how it behaves in the life. “A small bird in a steel cage with no door” tells you everything. The rules match.

Part 2

Finding the hub

Most of what someone says is background. Inside it is the word or phrase that holds the whole thing together — the hub.

Listen for three things:

Trap words. Locked, frozen, loop, spinning, heavy.

Crash points. The moment they describe wanting to do something and being stopped. “I want to try out, but there's a knot right here.”

Raw details. How it physically feels. “A jagged edge” is a hub. “Uncomfortable” is a summary.

Part 3

The ping

Your own body is a detector. When someone reaches a part of their map that matters, they feel it — a held breath, a tightening shoulder — and if you are quiet inside, you feel a small echo of it.

A tightening stomach. Sitting up straighter. Holding your own breath for half a second.

That is the ping: they just said the important word. Ask about that one.

It is not reliable at first, and it is not magic. It is attention, felt in the body.

Part 4

Four weeks

Don't practise on your friends yet. Practise on people who can't be affected by your mistakes.

Warm-up · the good thing

Before week one, and every week after. Find a video of someone talking about something they love — a place, a hobby, a dog. Track their words the same way. You are not looking for a problem; you are looking for what holds. It stops the tracking becoming a hunt.

Week 1 · the word

Podcast or vlog, story ignored. Listen only for the specific, odd nouns and adjectives they use for feelings. Don't paraphrase them in your head.

Week 2 · the space

An interview, sound on, eyes on the body. Every time they gesture about a feeling, draw a box in the air where they pointed. Watch the invisible map build around them.

Week 3 · the one word

Listen to someone complaining — a YouTuber, a character in a film. Ask yourself: if I could use the Magnifying Glass on one word from all of that, which word is the hub?

Week 4 · the room

The bus, the canteen, the dinner table. Just listen. No advice, no questions yet. Watch the map build, find the hub, notice whether you feel the ping.

When you can do all of that without trying, you are ready for the hard conversations — and you will not need to perform.
