Appendix A — The Translation Matrix

Bridging Clinical Clean Language & Pre-Teen Pedagogy This document serves as a "Rosetta Stone" for practitioners, educators, and parents. It maps the highly technical terminology of Clean Language, Symbolic Modelling, and Systemic NLP directly to the "Secret World" video-game metaphor designed for 10-to-12-year-olds.

By comparing these side-by-side, facilitators can ensure that the core epistemological rigor of the original methodologies remains completely intact while being communicated in a highly accessible format.

1. Foundational Epistemology &

Architecture Clinical / Technical Term The Secret World (10-12yo Metaphor)

Pedagogical Translation & Function Autogenic Narrative / Metaphor (Self-generated internal landscape)

The Secret Map Explains to kids that everyone

has a unique, custom-built level in their head. The player is the sole developer of this space.

Non-Contamination (Excluding facilitator's assumptions)

The "No Littering" Rule Teaches kids not to drop their

own "code," advice, or ideas into their friend's game. It frames unwanted advice as messing up someone else's save file.

Epistemological Distance (Perceptual distance from the phenomenon)

The Camera Angle Helps kids manage emotional

overwhelm. If a problem is too scary, they don't have to fight it; they just "zoom the camera out" to look at it safely from above.

Psychoactive Landscape (Spatial environment that responds to attention)

The World Engine / Live Map Teaches that the inner world

isn't a static drawing; it's a live video game engine. Just looking at a "glitch" with the right tool can cause the engine to automatically re- render and fix it.

2. Structural, Vector, & Spatial

Frameworks Clinical / Technical Term The Secret World (10-12yo Metaphor)

Pedagogical Translation & Function Node (An atomic metaphorical anchor/symptom)

The Landmark Converts abstract feelings (e.g., anxiety) into concrete, interactive game objects (e.g., a heavy rock, a dark cloud)

that can be located and modified.

Vector (Directional path or line of implication)

The Trail / Invisible Strings Helps kids track where a

feeling came from, where it is going, or how one Landmark is secretly "wired" to another.

Problem Loop (The Egg)

(Closed, self-referencing threat circuit)

The Glitch Normalizes getting stuck. It's

not that the child is "broken"; their game has just hit a repeating loop in the code that needs debugging.

Resource Space (The Chicken)

(Latent generative areas/strength)

The Safe Zone Reassures kids that no matter

how scary their map gets, the engine always hides a quiet, strong place with good code.

They just have to find it.

Somatic Endpoint (Physical manifestation of a structure)

Body Clues Teaches kids to watch for real- world physical updates (a deep sigh, relaxing shoulders) to know when the Inner Engine has successfully processed new code.

Binding / Double Binding (Two opposing forces locking the system)

The Glitch Trap Explains the feeling of being

paralyzed by two bad choices.

Teaches them not to force a choice, but to stare at the space between the traps to unlock it.

3. The Specialized Question Framework

(The Toolkit)

Clinical Intent & Clean Syntax The Secret World (10-12yo Metaphor)

Pedagogical Translation & Function Developing (Attributes)

"And what kind of [X] is that [X]?"

The Magnifying Glass Forces the inner graphics

engine to render high- resolution details of a confusing feeling.

Developing (Location)

"And whereabouts is that [X]?"

The Map Pin Stops feelings from being a

vague, overwhelming cloud by pinning them to a specific coordinate in their body or space.

Moving Attention "And is there anything else about [X]?"

The Flashlight Illuminates the dark edges

around a Landmark to discover hidden Safe Zones or hidden connections.

Temporal (Past)

"And what happens just before [X]?"

The Rewind Button Tracks the game footage

backward to find the exact moment the Glitch was triggered.

Temporal (Future)

"And then what happens?"

The Fast-Forward Button Tracks the code forward to see

what consequence the Glitch produces.

Outcome Pivot "And when [X], what would you like to have happen?"

The Quest Compass The ultimate course-correction

tool. Bypasses the Glitch by setting a completely new main objective for the game engine to solve.

4. Advanced Protocols & Systemic

Maturing Clinical / Technical Term The Secret World (10-12yo Metaphor)

Pedagogical Translation & Function Clean Space Protocol (Moving to multiple geographic coordinates)

The Teleporter Pad Teaches kids to physically walk

to a new spot in their bedroom to change their brain's perspective. Bypasses conscious "stuckness" via physical movement.

The Six Steps Architecture

(Iterative bypass of analytical resistance)

The Doodle Quest (Solo

Mode)

Adapts complex multi-stage tracking into a simple drawing exercise. Moving left to right on a page mirrors the six steps of tracking, pivoting, and integrating.

Landscape Maturing (Stage 5)

(Allowing structural shifts to integrate)

The Autosave Crucial pacing control.

Prevents kids from rushing away the moment they feel better. Teaches them to wait quietly while the brain "saves"

the new map over the old one.

Ecological Future Pacing (Testing the resource in future context)

The Portal Generator Asks the child what they will

notice tomorrow at school.

Plants the newly created Safe Zone code into their future timeline so it loads automatically.

Summary Note for Practitioners While the terminology of the "Secret World" is gamified, the underlying mechanics remain strictly systemic. By utilizing this matrix, a practitioner can confidently review a session conducted with a pre-teen and easily translate the child's "Autosave" or "Glitch Trap" back into formal clinical notes (Landscape Maturing and Double-Bind Resolution, respectively). This ensures that while the pedagogy is playful, the therapeutic or coaching container remains phenomenologically sound.
